Chapter 14

What I Wanted to Believe

One of the most difficult aspects of deception is that truth and falsehood rarely arrive as clearly separated realities. Most people imagine a choice between believing something that is obviously true or rejecting something that is obviously false. My experience was far more complicated. The relationship existed in a space where evidence and emotion constantly competed for influence, and neither seemed capable of completely overpowering the other.

By that point, I had accumulated enough concerns to justify serious questions. Certain discoveries continued troubling me. Some explanations felt incomplete. The future we had discussed for so long remained frustratingly out of reach. Despite all of that, the relationship itself continued moving forward, which created a conflict that was often exhausting to carry.

Part of me was investigating.

Part of me was hoping.

The two were no longer working together.

I remember spending evenings reviewing conversations that stretched back months. Messages that once made me smile now carried a different weight because I was reading them through the lens of uncertainty. Promises that had once inspired excitement now prompted reflection. Certain statements seemed different when viewed alongside the information I had uncovered.

More than once, I found myself scrolling through old conversations late into the night.

"Maybe I'm missing something," I would tell myself.

The thought felt reasonable.

After all, people make mistakes. Misunderstandings happen. Relationships encounter complications. There was still a part of me searching for an explanation that would allow everything to fit together again.

That search became its own form of emotional labor.

Every inconsistency seemed to require analysis. Every unanswered question demanded attention. Every new discovery had to be weighed against months of conversations, shared experiences, video calls, and emotional investment.

The deeper I went, the more difficult objectivity became.

I was not investigating a stranger.

I was investigating someone I cared about.

That distinction influenced everything.

When evidence challenges a relationship, people rarely evaluate that evidence in a neutral way. Emotions become involved. Memories become involved. Hopes become involved. The future a person has imagined becomes involved as well.

I had invested far more than money.

I had invested trust.

I had invested time.

I had invested belief.

Walking away from those investments felt far more difficult than acknowledging a few uncomfortable questions.

Looking back now, I understand that this is one of the reasons intelligent and capable people remain in situations long after warning signs begin appearing. The issue is rarely a lack of intelligence. The issue is emotional investment. Every sacrifice creates another reason to keep believing that the outcome will eventually justify the cost.

That reality shaped many of my decisions during that season.

Whenever new concerns emerged, another conversation would often provide temporary reassurance. A video call might restore confidence. A discussion about future plans might rekindle optimism. Words of affection might temporarily quiet the questions that had been growing louder in my mind.

The relief never lasted as long as it once had.

That was another change I began noticing.

Reassurance had become temporary.

Questions had become persistent.

The balance between trust and doubt was slowly shifting.

One evening, after another conversation that seemed designed to restore confidence, I sat quietly and stared at the screen long after the discussion had ended. The room was dark except for the glow of my phone, and the silence gave me space to think about something I had been avoiding.

What if I was spending more energy protecting the relationship than examining the truth?

The question unsettled me immediately.

For months, I had approached every concern with the goal of preserving what we had built. Every explanation had been given room to breathe. Every inconsistency had received patience. Every disappointment had been met with understanding.

What if I had been protecting hope instead of pursuing truth?

I did not have an answer.

At that moment, I was not ready for one.

Hope still occupied too much space within my heart.

The future still felt possible.

The promises still felt close enough to reach.

Yet something had undeniably changed.

The certainty that once carried me through every disappointment was fading.

In its place stood a growing awareness that the relationship might not be what I believed it to be.

That awareness did not arrive with a dramatic revelation.

It arrived quietly.

It arrived gradually.

It arrived one unanswered question at a time.

Years later, I would look back on that season and recognize it as the beginning of acceptance. The truth had not fully revealed itself yet, but the possibility of that truth had finally entered the conversation.

For a long time, I had focused on what I hoped was true.

Now I was beginning to confront what might be true.

The difference between those two realities would eventually change everything.
