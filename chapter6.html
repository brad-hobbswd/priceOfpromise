Chapter 6

The Meeting That Never Came

As the relationship continued developing, conversations about meeting in person became more frequent. What had once existed entirely through messages and video calls now seemed to be moving toward something tangible. For a long time, the relationship had lived inside screens, conversations, and promises. The possibility of finally standing face to face felt like the natural next step.

The anticipation grew steadily.

Every discussion about meeting transformed an idea into something that felt increasingly real. Dates were discussed. Possibilities were explored. Details were considered. Each conversation strengthened my belief that the day was finally approaching when the distance between us would disappear.

During that season, I had already planned a vacation with friends. The trip represented an opportunity to relax, recharge, and enjoy time with people I cared about. Another thought, however, remained quietly in the background throughout the planning process.

This trip could become much more than a vacation.

For the first time, there appeared to be a realistic opportunity to meet the person who had become such an important part of my life. Months of conversations had created a picture of what that meeting might be like. I imagined the first greeting. I imagined finally hearing a voice without a screen standing between us. I imagined sharing ordinary moments that would feel extraordinary simply because they were no longer taking place through a phone.

The plans felt real because everything surrounding them appeared real.

Dates had been discussed. Expectations had been built. Conversations about the future had made the meeting feel less like a possibility and more like an approaching reality. For the first time, the relationship seemed ready to step beyond messages and video calls into the physical world.

At some point, a request was made involving hotel arrangements connected to the planned meeting. Looking back now, that transaction would become one of the most significant decisions of the entire story. At the time, however, my perspective was very different.

I was not thinking about fraud. I was not thinking about manipulation. I was not thinking about deception. I was thinking about finally meeting someone I had come to trust and care about.

Trust changes the way people evaluate decisions. When confidence has been built through months of communication, shared experiences, video calls, and discussions about the future, certain requests can appear reasonable. Questions that might otherwise seem obvious become easier to overlook because the relationship itself appears authentic.

I gave the money.

In truth, I gave nearly everything I had available to give.

The decision felt significant, yet it also felt worthwhile. The meeting appeared close enough to touch. The future we had discussed for so long seemed ready to move from imagination into reality.

As the vacation unfolded, excitement remained close at hand. Friends surrounded me. New experiences filled the days. Beneath everything, however, another countdown was taking place.

Soon I would leave my friends behind.

Soon I would continue the journey alone.

Soon I would meet the man who had occupied so much of my life.

At least that was what I believed.

The closer the date arrived, the more real everything felt. The anticipation was difficult to describe because it carried the weight of months of conversations, expectations, and emotional investment. Every promise seemed to be leading toward this moment.

The day finally arrived.

The expectation grew.

The promise stood directly in front of me.

Then the promise disappeared.

The meeting never happened.

What remained was confusion, disappointment, and a growing collection of questions that I could not yet answer. At the time, I still believed there must be a reasonable explanation. Trust had been built over many months, and one failed meeting did not seem large enough to erase everything that had come before.

Looking back now, I recognize that this moment marked a turning point. The first major promise had failed to become reality, yet hope remained strong enough to keep me looking forward rather than looking backward.

That hope would prove far more costly than I understood.
