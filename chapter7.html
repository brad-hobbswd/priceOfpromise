Chapter 7

The Explanation

The meeting never happened, yet the relationship did not end.

Looking back, that fact may be difficult for some readers to understand. After everything that would eventually unfold, it might seem obvious that the missed meeting should have served as a warning. At the time, however, the situation felt far more complicated than it appears in hindsight.

Disappointment arrived immediately.

Questions arrived with it.

The anticipation that had been building for months suddenly had nowhere to go. Plans that had felt tangible only days earlier disappeared before they could become reality. A future event that had occupied so much of my attention was gone, leaving behind confusion and an uncomfortable sense of uncertainty.

Yet alongside those emotions came something else.

An explanation.

The explanation did not arrive wrapped in obvious deception. It arrived in a form that felt believable. Life is unpredictable. Emergencies happen. Unexpected obstacles appear. Most people have experienced moments when carefully arranged plans collapse because circumstances change without warning.

That reality made the explanation easier to accept.

Trust influences how people interpret disappointment. When trust is strong, people naturally search for reasons to preserve the relationship rather than reasons to abandon it. They give the benefit of the doubt. They extend patience. They choose understanding instead of suspicion.

That was exactly what happened.

I wanted to believe there was a legitimate reason the meeting had failed to take place. More importantly, I had no compelling reason not to believe it. Months of conversations, video calls, and emotional connection continued outweighing a single disappointment.

The relationship had not been built upon one planned meeting. The relationship had been built upon hundreds of conversations. It had been built upon trust, consistency, and the belief that two people were moving toward the same future.

Because of that foundation, hope survived.

The disappointment remained painful, yet the possibility of a future meeting appeared very much alive. New discussions emerged. New plans were mentioned. New opportunities were explored. The promise that had disappeared seemed to reappear in a different form, once again occupying a place on the horizon.

That pattern would become familiar.

A plan would emerge. Excitement would grow. An obstacle would appear. An explanation would follow. A new promise would replace the old one.

At the time, however, I did not see a pattern.

I saw possibility.

That distinction matters.

Patterns become visible when people possess enough distance to examine events objectively. During the experience itself, emotions often prevent that perspective from forming. Each situation feels separate from the one before it. Each explanation feels connected to a unique set of circumstances. Each new promise feels independent of previous disappointments.

Hope encourages that way of thinking.

Hope whispers that next time will be different. Hope suggests that delays are temporary. Hope insists that what did not happen today may still happen tomorrow.

The relationship continued moving forward under the influence of those expectations. Conversations remained frequent. Video calls remained reassuring. Future plans remained part of our discussions. From the outside, little appeared to have changed.

Something had changed, however.

A promise had been broken.

The crack was small enough that I barely noticed it.

Trust remained intact.

Optimism remained strong.

The relationship continued.

Yet somewhere beneath the surface, the first fracture had already formed.

At the time, I could not see it.

Years later, I would understand that the fracture appeared long before the collapse.

The missed meeting was not the end of the story.

In many ways, it was the beginning of a cycle that would repeat itself more times than I ever imagined.
