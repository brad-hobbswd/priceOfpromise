Chapter 19

The Call

By the time I contacted Robinhood, my life felt increasingly crowded with questions.

The relationship still occupied a significant place in my heart, yet financial concerns were beginning to surface in ways I could not fully understand. Account issues were appearing. Transactions that once seemed ordinary were creating complications. Questions seemed to be multiplying faster than answers, and the uncertainty was becoming difficult to ignore.

Hoping to find some clarity, I picked up the phone and called Robinhood.

At first, the conversation felt routine. After navigating the automated prompts and waiting on hold, a representative joined the call and introduced himself. His voice was calm, professional, and patient.

"Thank you for calling Robinhood. How can I help you today?"

I explained the situation as clearly as I could. I described the concerns I was having and asked why my account was experiencing problems.

"I've been trying to understand what's happening," I said. "Something doesn't seem right, and I can't figure out why."

The representative listened carefully.

"I understand your concern," he replied. "Let me review the account information available to me."

Several moments passed while he reviewed whatever information appeared on his screen. The silence felt longer than it probably was. I sat staring at the wall in front of me, hoping the conversation would finally provide answers.

Instead, it provided something entirely different.

"There are some things I'm not able to discuss," he explained. "Certain account decisions are not information I can disclose."

My frustration immediately grew.

"So you're telling me the account is affected, but you can't tell me why?"

"I understand that's frustrating," he said. "There are limitations regarding what I can share."

The answer felt incomplete.

I had called seeking clarity and seemed to be moving farther away from it.

As the conversation continued, the representative began asking questions of his own.

Some focused on transactions.

Others focused on circumstances surrounding those transactions.

Then the conversation shifted again.

"May I ask you a personal question?" he said.

The request caught me off guard.

"Sure."

"Have you been communicating with someone online?"

I remember pausing before answering.

"Yes."

"Is this someone you've met in person?"

The question immediately made me uncomfortable.

"No."

Another brief silence followed.

"Has this person ever requested money from you or asked you to assist with financial matters?"

The room suddenly felt smaller.

I shifted in my chair.

"Why are you asking me that?"

The representative paused for a moment before answering.

"Based on some of the situations you've described, I think it's important to ask whether you may be involved in what is commonly referred to as a romance scam."

The words seemed to echo in my mind.

A romance scam.

For several seconds, I said nothing.

I remember staring straight ahead while the phrase settled over me.

Romance scams happened to other people. Romance scams were stories on television. Romance scams were warnings shared online. The relationship I had spent months building did not belong in that category.

At least that was what I wanted to believe.

"I don't think that's what's happening," I finally replied.

The representative's tone remained respectful.

"I understand. Many situations are unique. I'm simply encouraging you to examine the circumstances carefully."

The conversation continued for several more minutes, yet my attention remained fixed on those two words.

Romance scam.

When the call finally ended, I sat alone in silence.

The representative had not provided every answer I wanted. He had not explained precisely why the account had been affected. He had not told me whether the account issues were connected to other problems beginning to emerge throughout my financial life.

What he had given me was something far more unsettling.

A possibility.

As the days passed, that possibility refused to leave me alone.

I thought about the missed meeting.

I thought about the money.

I thought about the promises.

I thought about the explanations that always seemed to arrive whenever disappointment appeared.

The questions I had been carrying for months suddenly felt heavier because they were no longer mine alone.

Someone outside the relationship had seen enough to raise a warning.

That realization followed me everywhere.

Later, additional concerns would emerge. Credit files would be frozen. Protective measures would be placed around my Social Security number. Account issues would continue appearing, and questions surrounding financial activity would become increasingly difficult to ignore. Throughout that season, I often wondered whether those events were connected to one another.

No one ever provided a complete answer.

No one ever connected every piece of the puzzle.

The uncertainty remained.

The warning, however, remained as well.

Looking back now, I can see that the phone call marked a turning point. Until then, most of the warnings had come from inside my own mind. Doubt had been competing against hope, and hope usually found a way to win.

This time was different.

The warning came from someone with no emotional investment in my future.

The warning came from someone who had nothing to gain by saying it.

The warning came from outside the story I had been telling myself.

When I hung up the phone that day, one question continued repeating itself.

What if he was right?

For the first time, I found myself unable to dismiss that possibility.

The truth had not fully arrived yet.

The truth, however, no longer felt far away.
